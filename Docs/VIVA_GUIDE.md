# Legacy of the Temple: viva guide

Everything here comes from the real project (Unity 6000.6.8f1, URP, C#; `Resources/Story/story.json` version 3; the scripts in `Assets/_Project/Scripts`). Items marked **Check before the viva** are uncertain or depend on files whose origin is not recorded.

## 1. Two-minute introduction (read aloud)
> "Good morning, sir. My project is **Legacy of the Temple**, a short third-person mystery and action game made in **Unity 6** with the **Universal Render Pipeline** and **C#**.
>
> The story: on the night before a temple festival in a fictional Indian village called Suryapur, five temple treasures are stolen. I play Aryan, an investigator, and I have until dawn to bring them back. On the way I find four small clues that show the door was opened from the inside, by Madhav, the friendly key-keeper.
>
> The game has six places around the temple: a bazaar, the storeroom, underground vaults, a stepwell, a Sun Wall and a river road. Each place has its own **physical puzzle**: carrying pot shards, placing a lantern, pushing stone blocks, turning stone discs, filling and draining water, and bending sunlight with mirrors. Each place ends with a **short fight**, and the last part is a **jeep chase**. A **Dawn Clock** counts down to sunrise and decides the ending.
>
> Technically, the whole world is one Unity scene. The village, roads and puzzle areas are **generated by C# code** when the scene loads. The story is a **JSON data file**. The puzzles use a small **framework I can test automatically**: 15 play-mode tests pass. A **test bot** plays the whole game from New Game to the ending with real key presses. I used **Blender** for the jeep and puzzle props, **Mixamo** for animations, **Cinemachine and Timeline** for the intro, and an **audio mixer** for sound.
>
> My role was the design, the direction and the testing. I used an AI coding assistant to help write code and build assets under my direction, and I tested and can explain every part. Thank you; I will now show the game."

## 2. Short script for each area (30 to 45 seconds each)

**Intro and arrival.**
- "The game starts with a cutscene made with Cinemachine cameras on a Timeline, built in code (`IntroCinematic.cs`). Aryan drives in, visible in the driver's seat.
- The jeep follows a smooth path without physics, so it never jitters; physics only starts when the player drives.
- Then I walk through the village gate and the temple appears: it is hidden on the approach and revealed with a camera move and a banner."

**Bazaar (stealth).**
- "A courier carries the first treasure, a bell, and the bell rings when he moves. I have to follow him without a marker.
- He stops and looks back; if I am close, in the open and not crouching, he spots me. After three times he runs, but he always ends in the same yard, so I can never get stuck.
- This teaches stealth and listening. In code it is a line-of-sight check in `BazaarTail.cs`, and the bell sound is generated in code."

**Storeroom (broken pot and lantern).**
- "This puzzle rebuilds the break-in from objects. Three pot shards lie around. I carry each one to the plinth; when the pot is whole it holds oil and the lantern lights.
- I place the lantern on the oil stain, which frees the loose flagstone. The lock plate turns and shows scratches on the inside, the first clue to the inside man.
- In code the shards are `Carryable` objects, the plinth has three `PlacementSocket`s, and a `PuzzleGroup` checks the solution. It is in the game so the player learns by doing, not by reading a clue text."

**Vaults (blocks and discs).**
- "First I push two stone blocks onto two pressure plates to open a gate; one block must go round a pillar, and a lever resets them.
- Then three stone discs must match the rings of light that a roof grate draws on the floor. The answer is never written; you have to notice the light.
- In code: `PushableBlock`, `PressurePlate`, `RotatingDisc`, and the answer {2, 5, 3} in `VaultPuzzle.cs`."

**Stepwell (water).**
- "It is night and raining. Two sluice wheels fill the tank and lift a raft. But full water hides the secret panel, so I drain it back to the carved middle mark, where the raft meets the panel.
- The lesson: the obvious move is not the answer. In code a `WaterBasin` holds the level, `WaterValve`s change it, and the raft follows the level."

**Sun Wall (light and mirrors).**
- "Four bronze mirrors bend two sunbeams onto two carvings. One beam is blocked by a spoke of a giant stone wheel, so I push the wheel one stop.
- In code each `LightMirror` reflects a ray from a `LightBeamEmitter`; a `LightSocket` lights when the ray hits it. Then I fight Kishore, a mini boss with two phases."

**River road and ruins (chase and final fight).**
- "I chase Bhaskar in the jeep. The jeep uses `WheelCollider`s with suspension and grip. If I fall behind he slows down, so the chase cannot be lost.
- At the ruins there are two waves of robbers and then Bhaskar in three phases. The fight system (`Brawl.cs`) allows at most two attackers at once, telegraphs attacks, and has block, parry, dodge and a slow-motion finisher.
- If I named Madhav correctly on the evidence board, the second wave is smaller."

**Ending.**
- "I return the treasures to the priest. The ending screen shows the five treasures, the evidence, a grade per place, the Dawn Clock, the score and the rank in the best-runs table.
- If I finish before 05:45, the ending is 'Dawn Light'; otherwise it is 'Late Dawn'."

## 3. Question and answer bank

### Game design
1. **Why a heritage mystery?** To show Indian temple culture in a respectful, fictional story. The treasures and the village are fictional, and the real shrine is only a background element that is never touched.
2. **Why physical puzzles instead of talking?** Design pillar: "every answer is earned by doing something" (carry, fit, turn, push). It is more memorable than reading a clue.
3. **Why the broken pot and the lantern?** They rebuild the crime: the robber's lamp stood on the spilled oil. Putting the lantern back on the stain shows the loose stone, so the player discovers how the break-in happened.
4. **Why a ticking clock?** Tension without a game-over screen. It changes the ending instead.
5. **How do you stop the player getting stuck?** Soft fails everywhere: puzzles reset, lost fights restart on the spot, the courier always ends in the same yard, and Bhaskar slows down if you fall behind. H gives three hints.
6. **Why so little dialogue?** The world tells the story through objects, light and sound; there are 29 short spoken lines in total.
7. **How long is the game?** About 20 to 30 minutes. The test bot finishes it in about 8 minutes.
8. **What is the twist?** The inside man is Madhav, the key-keeper. Four clues point to him: a list, the lock scratched inside, a ledger signed "M.", and a key ring.

### Unity
9. **What is a GameObject?** Any object in a scene. Components (lights, colliders, scripts) give it behaviour.
10. **What is a prefab?** A saved GameObject you can reuse, for example the jeep, the villagers and the pot.
11. **Collider vs trigger?** A collider blocks; a trigger only reports that something entered it.
12. **What is a Rigidbody?** It lets the physics engine move an object. The jeep has one; the player does not.
13. **Why a CharacterController for the player?** It moves predictably, slides along walls and climbs steps without physics forces pushing it around.
14. **What is the Animator?** It plays and blends animation clips. Idle, walk and run blend by speed in a blend tree.
15. **What is a Humanoid avatar?** A standard bone map, so Mixamo clips can drive any human character (retargeting).
16. **Do you use NavMesh?** No. NPCs walk scripted paths. It was simpler; there is no deeper reason.
17. **What is URP?** The Universal Render Pipeline: Unity's fast renderer that runs on most hardware and supports post-processing Volumes.
18. **What is a Volume?** An area with post-processing settings (colour grade, bloom, vignette). Each place has its own Volume, so each has its own mood.
19. **What is a Canvas?** The UI layer for menus and HUD.
20. **Which input system?** Unity's new Input System package: keyboard, mouse and gamepad in one place.
21. **What are Cinemachine and Timeline?** Cinemachine gives smart virtual cameras; Timeline is a sequencer that switches them. I used them for the intro.
22. **What is a ScriptableObject, and do you use one?** A data asset. The story is a JSON file instead (`story.json`), read by `StoryData.cs`.
23. **How do you build the game?** A build script, `AuditBuildTools.Build`, makes `Builds/LegacyOfTheTemple.app` for macOS. Development build, 0 errors and 0 warnings.
24. **How do you save?** PlayerPrefs (Unity's small key-value store): story step, puzzle states, checkpoints, clock and scores.

### C# (see section 4 for line by line)
25. **What does `PuzzleElement` do?** It is the base of every puzzle part. It holds one whole-number state, saves it, shows it and tells listeners when it changes.
26. **What does `PuzzleGroup` do?** It checks whether every part is in its target state, then marks the puzzle solved, locks the parts, adds score and plays a sound.
27. **What is an event in C#?** A list of functions to call when something happens, for example `Changed` on a puzzle part.

### Blender
28. **What did you make in Blender?** The jeep (52,778 triangles, LODs, three damage states), the break-in pot and its shards, and the V3 props (pillar, coping, plinth, lamp stand, arch), all from Python scripts in `Tools/blender`.
29. **How do you export to Unity?** FBX, at metre scale, with the forward axis and smoothing set. Materials are rebuilt as URP materials in Unity.
30. **What are UVs?** The 2D layout of a mesh's surface, so a texture knows where to go.
31. **What is an LOD?** Level of detail: simpler versions of a mesh used far away (the jeep has 3; the temple has per-area LODs).

### Animation
32. **Where are the animations from?** Mixamo (idle, walk, run, crouch, jump) and Open Kit 3D (sword moves), retargeted through Humanoid avatars.
33. **How did you stop foot sliding?** The blend value matches the real speed, and the clip playback rate follows the ground speed (walk 4 m/s at ×1.0, run 7.5 m/s at ×1.77).

### Graphics
34. **How is the lighting done?** One real-time sun and fog that blend to each place's time of day (midday, night, dusk), plus point lights for lamps and torches and one reflection probe per place. No baked lighting, because the world is built at run time.
35. **What post-processing?** ACES tonemapping, exposure, white balance, contrast, saturation, bloom, vignette and grain, per place.

### Audio
36. **How is sound organised?** An audio mixer with music, ambience, effects and voice groups; the music ducks under voices. Footsteps change by surface. 1,238 sound plays of 29 clips were counted in one test run.

### Performance
37. **What was the biggest performance problem?** The CPU render thread took 17 to 40 ms a frame. Thousands of blocks used per-object property blocks, which break URP's SRP Batcher. Baking them into shared materials brought every place under 9 ms on average.
38. **What is the biggest single cost now?** The temple model: 1.87 million triangles (LOD1 1.38 million).

### Problems and fixes
39. **A bug you fixed?** The hero always played the Running clip in slow motion, because the code sent metres per second to a 0-to-1 blend tree. I mapped the speed back to 0..1, so walking and running now look right.
40. **Another?** The ending always said "Late Dawn" in V3, because it still checked a flag from the old story. I fixed the rule so finishing before 05:45 gives "Dawn Light".
41. **Another?** Skipping the intro threw the jeep into the air; the driver is now taken out of the seat before collisions return.

### AI use (honest)
42. **Did you use AI?** "Yes. An AI coding assistant helped write code, Blender scripts and documents, under my direction. I decided the design, checked every result in the running game and in test reports, and I can explain every part of it."

### Teamwork and future
43. **Who did what?** **Check before the viva:** state your own role and any team members truthfully.
44. **What would you improve next?** See `Docs/NEXT_STEPS.md`: real village-thief robber models, face rigs for all characters, more environment art instead of textured blocks, kick and grab animations, controller polish and a Windows build.

## 4. Script walkthrough (five key scripts)

### 4.1 `Characters/PlayerController.cs`: moving the hero
- `walkSpeed = 4.0f; runSpeed = 7.5f; acceleration = 28f;` The speeds in metres per second, and how fast the hero reaches them.
- Reads W A S D or the stick; `if (kb.capsLockKey.wasPressedThisFrame) AutoRun = !AutoRun;` toggles auto-run.
- `moveDir = (camFwd * inputDir.z + camRight * inputDir.x).normalized;` Movement is relative to the camera, so W always goes "into the screen".
- `CurrentSpeed = Mathf.MoveTowards(CurrentSpeed, targetSpeed, acceleration * Time.deltaTime);` Quick, smooth acceleration.
- `characterController.Move(finalMove * Time.deltaTime);` Moves the capsule; gravity is added to the vertical speed.
- `animator.SetFloat("Speed", blend, 0.08f, Time.deltaTime);` Tells the blend tree how fast we go (0..1). The playback rate is set from the clips' real speed, so the feet do not slide.

### 4.2 `Gameplay/Puzzles/PuzzleElement.cs`: one puzzle part
- `public int State { get; private set; }` Each part (disc, lever, socket) is just one number.
- `State = PuzzleSave.Get(SaveId, startState);` Restores the saved state when the game loads.
- `SetState(...)`: changes the number, saves it, plays the sound, calls `Apply()` to move the object, and fires `Changed`.
- `protected abstract void Apply(int state, bool instant);` Each part type decides how to show its state (rotate a disc, lift a lever).

### 4.3 `Gameplay/Puzzles/PuzzleGroup.cs`: one puzzle
- `List<Condition> conditions` is the list of "this part must be in this state".
- `ConditionsMet()` returns false if any part is wrong.
- `Evaluate()` runs when any part changes. If everything is right: mark solved, save, lock the parts, `ScoreSystem.PuzzleSolved(...)`, play a success sound, fire `OnSolved`.
- `NextHint()` gives hint 1, 2, then 3; each new hint is counted against the score.

### 4.4 `Gameplay/BreakInPuzzle.cs`: the storeroom
- `Build()` makes the storeroom from boxes plus the Blender pot. It creates three `Carryable` shards, three `PlacementSocket`s, the lantern (a `Carryable` with a `Light`), a `ProximityMatch` for the oil stain, the lock plate (`RotatingDisc`, 4 stops) and the flagstone (`PuzzleLever`).
- `Group.conditions.Add(...)`: solved when the three sockets are full and the flagstone is up.
- `Refresh()`: when all three sockets are full, show the whole pot and light the lantern; unlock the flagstone only when the lantern is on the stain; lock plate at stop 2 records the evidence; lifting the flagstone starts the lookout fight (`Brawl.Begin`).

### 4.5 `Gameplay/Brawl.cs`: the fights
- `Brawl.Begin(zone, centre, radius, robbers, onWon)` spawns the robbers in a circle and starts the fight.
- `MaxAttackers = 2` means only two robbers may attack at the same time (attack tokens); the others circle.
- `ReadInput()`: left click strike, R heavy (costs stamina), V dodge (costs stamina), Q hold block, F finisher on a staggered robber.
- Enemies are a small state machine: approach, circle, telegraph (a visible wind-up), strike, recover, staggered, down.
- When all are down, `OnWon` is called and the zone gives its treasure. If the player loses, the fight restarts on the spot.

## 5. Five-minute demo plan
**Before the viva:**
- Start the built game `Builds/LegacyOfTheTemple.app` (a development build, so the **F9 demo menu** works).
- In a release build, turn on Pause > Settings > "Demo mode (F9 menu)".
- Have the Unity project open as a backup.

| Time | Do | Say |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | New Game; let the intro play 15 s, then hold Esc | "Cinemachine and Timeline intro; the jeep follows a path without physics." |
| 0:30 | Walk and run up the road (Shift), tap Caps Lock | "CharacterController movement, 4 and 7.5 m/s, animation matched to speed." |
| 1:00 | Through the gate: the temple appears | "Temple reveal; 1.87 million-triangle model with LODs." |
| 1:20 | F9 > 3 Storeroom; pick up a shard, place it on the plinth; press H once | "Carry and place; a PuzzleGroup checks the solution; three hints." |
| 2:00 | F9 > P (complete step), then F9 > 4 Vaults; turn one disc | "The disc answer is drawn by the light on the floor." |
| 2:30 | F9 > 6 Sun Wall; turn one mirror | "Light beams bounce off mirrors to sockets." |
| 3:00 | F9 > 8 Ruin court: the final fight starts | "Brawl system: two attackers at most, block, parry, dodge, finisher." |
| 3:45 | Fight a little; F9 > U if health is low | "Retry on the spot if you lose." |
| 4:15 | M: show the map and fast travel | "Fast travel only to places you have reached; costs 2 Dawn Clock minutes." |
| 4:40 | Esc > Controls; then quit | "Settings: graphics presets, volumes, subtitles, difficulty." |

**If something goes wrong:**
- If the game crashes or hangs, restart it, press **Continue** (the story step is saved), and use F9 again.
- If the build does not start, open `Assets/_Project/Scenes/Dev/MainMenu.unity` in Unity and press Play.
- As a last resort, play `Docs/QA/full_run.mov` (the whole game, 4 min 31 s).

## 6. One-page cheat sheet
- **Engine:** Unity 6000.6.8f1, URP, C#, new Input System, Cinemachine and Timeline. **Tools:** Blender 5 (Python scripts), Mixamo, macOS `say` for voices.
- **Code:** about 21,000 lines of runtime C# in 5 assemblies (Core, Characters, World, Gameplay, UI); 15 automated play-mode tests; a whole-game test bot.
- **World:** one scene; six places around the temple. The ruin court is about 500 m from the arrival point.
- **Story:** 9 steps, 5 treasures, 4 pieces of evidence, 29 spoken lines, 3 endings (Dawn Light, Late Dawn, Lost Dawn).
- **Puzzles (5):** bazaar tail; storeroom (pot, lantern, lock plate, flagstone); vaults (blocks and discs, answer 2 5 3); stepwell (water level 2); Sun Wall (mirrors two stops each, wheel one stop).
- **Fights:** courier and helper, lookout, two guards, knife man and helper, Kishore (2 phases), wave 1 (3), wave 2 (1 or 3), Bhaskar (3 phases).
- **Numbers:**
  - Temple: 1.87 M triangles. Jeep: 52,778 triangles with 3 LODs.
  - Walk 4 m/s, run 7.5 m/s.
  - Dawn Clock 03:10 to 05:45.
  - Every place above 60 fps on High (slowest place 8.5 ms average).
- **Controls:**
  - Movement: WASD move, Shift run, Caps Lock auto-run, C crouch.
  - Actions: E act, H hints, B evidence board, J journal, M map and fast travel.
  - Fighting: left click strike, R heavy, Q block, V dodge, F finish.
  - Jeep: W S A D, Space brake, L lights, G horn.
  - F9 demo menu.
- **Key terms:** prefab, collider, trigger, Rigidbody, CharacterController, Animator, blend tree, Humanoid retargeting, URP Volume, SRP Batcher, LOD, PlayerPrefs, JSON story data, WheelCollider.
