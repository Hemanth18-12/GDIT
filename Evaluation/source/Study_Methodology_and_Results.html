1. Method

1.1 Design

The evaluation has two parts: functional verification of the current game systems, and a planned single-session study with an after-play measure. The player study will examine understanding of puzzle ideas, usability and attitudes towards protecting heritage. Technical test passes will be reported separately from participant responses.

1.2 Participants and ethics

No real participant-response dataset was supplied for this report. Recruitment is planned among consenting adults aged 18 or above. Each participant should read the existing information sheet and sign the consent form, with separate optional permission for screenshots or video. Participation is voluntary and may stop at any time. Report the actual recruited, excluded and analysed counts after collection; do not treat the example-answer document as collected data.

1.3 Procedure

  1. Explain the study, confirm eligibility and consent, and assign a participant code. Keep any roll-number linkage separate from published results.
  2. Provide a short controls tutorial, then a suggested 30-minute route sampling courtyard reconstruction, Sun Wall mirrors, stepwell water and evidence deduction. Record which tasks each participant reaches, help given, retries and barriers.
  3. Complete the after-play form: six knowledge questions, confidence, learning-habit items, ten game-rating statements, a preference question and two open questions.
  4. Score answers and summarise ratings and comments. Questions tied to unseen tasks must be identified when interpreting outcomes.

1.4 Measures

Each knowledge item is worth one point (total 0–6). Confidence and agreement statements use a 1–5 scale. Report correct counts per item, score mean, median and range, and agreement counts (4–5). For a small convenience sample, use descriptive summaries and explain missing answers.

3. What the Results Mean

What is supported

Wider Editor PlayMode run: 32/32 passed. Final focused original-route run: 5/5 passed. These are separate, overlapping runs; their counts are not added. The focused tests preserve the established temple footprint and check the sealed doorway, real mirror rays, wheel obstruction, R-key vehicle recovery, local retry and independent audio settings. The E regression measures posed mesh and head positions throughout an actual task interaction; collision alone would miss the reported visual dip.

Changes made in the implementation

The main game preserves the original village, Courtyard and Sun Wall locations confirmed by the team. The detailed temple game prefab and original footprint were restored, while the unwanted Courtyard doorway stays sealed. The minimap now follows the established roads; R repairs the Jeep locally; same-scene retries restore the local clock budget. Five separate volume sliders sit in a scrolling pause menu. An existing licensed interaction clip replaces the empty E animation state.

What still needs observation

The rebuilt app screenshots show the rendered minimap and original places. Focused tests exercise actual E and R input; the local retry check invokes the recovery method. Reproducible QA positions the player between locations and samples selected cases. It does not establish every possible path or camera angle. Audio bindings do not establish perceived clarity or musical quality. Saved Master/Music settings were 0%; raise them for an audible demonstration.

Limitations

There is no analysed player sample, baseline score or comparison group. An after-play study can show understanding after play but cannot establish a learning gain caused by the game. A short route may not expose every participant to all six knowledge topics. Automated gameplay covers selected paths, while screenshot inspection samples a few views. Keeping the complete temple game mesh costs more geometry than the damaged reduced LODs. No new controlled FPS, memory or build-size benchmark is claimed. The game is heritage-inspired fiction rather than a verified reconstruction.

4. Next Step

Complete an adult pilot study using the existing information sheet, consent form and after-play questionnaire. Check the wording and answer keys, record each participant’s task exposure, then fill the tables with real counts, scores and comments. Improve unclear objectives or puzzle feedback and repeat the affected checks.

For a later pre/post study, validate the baseline Set A design in source/make_forms.py, make both forms available and use a matching participant code in each. The current local Apps Script creates the after-play form only. A larger, more varied sample and a text-based comparison condition would support stronger conclusions.
