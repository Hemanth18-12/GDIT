We are Hemanth (S20240010236) · Aakash (S20240010245) · Sai Teja (S20240010136). Legacy of the Temple is a desktop, third-person Unity game inspired by Odisha temple architecture. It is not a validated XR reconstruction. The original slide template supplies the typography, gold and brown palette and illustrative cover art; that artwork is not a game screenshot. SLIDE 2
The project explores whether active observation and solving a problem can make a heritage theme more engaging. This is a design motivation, not an established learning outcome. The planned study measures both puzzle understanding and player experience. In this game, a clue has a purpose: it supports a decision or opens a path. Dialogue connects that action with the story of a community protecting its memory. The recovered vessel, rosette and manuscript are fictional, and the shrine idol is a background element that the player never handles. We chose investigation because it gives the player a reason to look carefully. That is our design hypothesis. We still need real participant responses to know whether the tasks are readable and whether the heritage message comes through. We will use six transfer questions, ratings and open comments rather than assume that a completed puzzle automatically means understanding.

SLIDE 3
The player explores, inspects clues, solves environmental puzzles, resolves an encounter and recovers objects. Evidence connects dialogue and puzzle actions to the story. In the intended ending, the stolen pieces return to their community; the message is protecting memory and heritage. Use the live game to demonstrate the current encounter implementation. For example, inspecting the Courtyard supplies a clue, and solving a reconstruction task changes what becomes possible next. Different zones vary the physical action while keeping the investigation loop familiar. The minimap, prompts and checkpoint recovery are intended to help the player remain focused on the current task. The six-zone automated run checks selected routes, interactions, encounters and recovery conditions, but it does not replace a first-time player. A person may misunderstand a prompt even when the underlying system works. Our proposed after-play study asks about next-objective clarity, control difficulty and puzzle understanding so that feedback can point to a specific part of the loop.

SLIDE 6
The Jeep is a movement and navigation system for the road sections. It uses Unity PhysX wheel colliders, which represent wheel contact and suspension behaviour. Recovery is important because a flipped or stuck vehicle can interrupt the investigation. Pressing R calls repair and road reset: damage state is cleared, orientation returns upright, and the vehicle is repositioned on a road location. The upgrade test begins from a deliberately tilted position, calls that method, and checks that the vehicle is upright and damage is zero. The zone scenario also exercises Jeep entry, driving and exit. These checks cover selected behaviour; a method call does not prove every keyboard context, collision or possible recovery location. The editable diagram shows the intended state transition, not fabricated before-and-after screenshots. During a live demo, demonstrate a safe recovery on an open road and confirm that movement resumes.

SLIDE 8
The soundtrack aims for a calm devotional-style atmosphere that supports investigation. The implementation summary describes regenerated temple music and ambience, with melodic textures, bells and dawn birds. These are game soundscapes, not a claim to reproduce an authenticated ritual recording or a particular raga. The interface has five volume controls: master, music, ambience, sound effects and voice. This lets a player reduce music while keeping dialogue or effects audible. Subtitles and voice-ready dialogue hooks also support understanding. The upgrade test sets selected runtime volume values and confirms that they reach the audio systems. That is a technical state check rather than a listening study or a complete test of every slider gesture. During the demo, briefly vary music and voice, then restore comfortable levels. The pending player questionnaire asks whether voices and subtitles are clear and whether the game’s presentation helps players follow the task.
