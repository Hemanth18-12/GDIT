Description: You are invited to take part in a research study about how an educational video game can help people learn to observe, reason from evidence and use simple science ideas, and how it can make cultural heritage feel close and worth protecting. We want to understand how players experience the game, how easy it is to use, how engaging it is, and whether it helps them understand the ideas.

The game is set in Suryapur, a fictional Indian temple village, on the night before the temple festival. Five fictional temple treasures have been stolen. You play Aryan, an investigator, who must recover them before dawn. With help from Pujari Ananda and Meera, a village girl, you solve puzzles in places such as a storeroom, underground vaults, an old stepwell and a sun-lit wall. Each puzzle uses an idea such as light and shadow, reflection by mirrors, water levels, planning moves or reasoning from clues. There are short, non-graphic fights with robbers and one jeep chase. The temple's sacred idol appears only as a respectful background and is never part of the play.

During the study you will:

  • fill in a short online form about yourself, your gaming habits and your current knowledge (about 5 minutes);
  • play the game on our laptop with a keyboard and mouse (about 30 minutes);
  • fill in a second short form with a few knowledge questions and your opinion of the game (about 5 minutes);
  • if you wish, tell us about your experience at the end.

While you play, the researcher notes the places you reach, the hints you use and the score on the game's ending screen. The game itself does not record your name, face or voice. Your data is linked only to a participant ID (for example P001) and stays on our laptop. Your name appears only on the consent form, which is stored separately.

Your participation in this research is voluntary. You may choose not to participate or withdraw from the study at any time without penalty or consequences. You may also request a break at any time, may refuse to answer any question, and may ask us to delete your data by giving us your participant ID.

The results will be used for our academic project and may be reported in presentations or papers. They will be reported only in anonymised or combined form; your name and participant ID will never appear in the results.

Please do not hesitate to inform the researcher if you have any questions, discomfort, or would like to take a break during the experiment.

Duration: Your participation will take approximately 40–45 minutes.

We sincerely appreciate your involvement and valuable feedback, and we thank you for your participation.
